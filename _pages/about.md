## Introducation

Hongli Hu is a professor in School of Basic Medical Sciences, Division of Life Sciences and Medicine, University of Sciences and Technology of China (USTC), and a Principal Investigator in the State Key Laboratory of Immune Response and Immunotherapy, USTC. Dr. Hu received his B.S. degree in College of Veterinary Medicine from Nanjing Agricultural University in 2012 and his Ph.D. degree in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology from the Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, in 2018. He completed his postdoctoral training at Harvard Medical School / Boston Children’s Hospital from 2019 to 2025 under the guide of Prof. Fred Alt. In April 2021, he was promoted as an Instructor in Pediatrics at Harvard Medical School. He moved to the USTC, Hefei, China in June, 2025 and established the molecular immunology laboratory since then. 

His lab focuses on the regulation of antibody diversity and genomic instability, escepically regulatory mechanism during V(D)J recombination. Integrating techniques such as proteomics, metabolomics and bioinformatics, our goals are promoting the improvement of theories and strategies for antibody and vaccine design and antibody-dependent treatments. As co-first or co-corresponding author, he has published several research articles in journals including  ***Nature*** (2021, 2024, 2026), ***Nature Cell Biology*** (2018) and ***Hepatology*** (2017). He also served as a reviewer for international academic journals such as Frontiers and Fundamental Research.


## Research Interests

Our research will focus on the regulatory networks governing early B cell development, addressing key scientific questions such as chromatin spatial conformational changes and the regulation of loop extrusion. We aim to elucidate the mechanisms by which cis- and trans-regulatory elements, transcription factors, and the microenvironment contribute to the generation of antibody diversity, as well as their applicability in human disease. This work will provide theoretical support for vaccine design and antibody engineering research. Additionally, we will further explore the therapeutic targets and intervention strategies for diseases related to aberrant antibody gene recombination, such as autoimmune disorders.

# Optimization of antibody model animals, vaccine design, and antibody engineering;
# Research on tumors, autoimmune diseases and new targets screening.
