## Introducation

Hongli Hu is a professor in School of Basic Medical Sciences, Division of Life Sciences and Medicine, University of Sciences and Technology of China (USTC), and a Principal Investigator in the State Key Laboratory of Immune Response and Immunotherapy, USTC. Dr. Hu received his B.S. degree in College of Veterinary Medicine from Nanjing Agricultural University in 2012 and his Ph.D. degree in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology from the Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences, in 2018. He completed his postdoctoral training at Harvard Medical School / Boston Children’s Hospital from 2019 to 2025 under the guide of Prof. Fred Alt. In April 2021, he was promoted as an Instructor in Pediatrics at Harvard Medical School. He moved to the USTC, Hefei, China in June, 2025 and established the molecular immunology laboratory. 

His lab focuses on the regulation of antibody diversity and genomic instability, escepically regulatory mechanism during V(D)J recombination. As co-first or co-corresponding author, he has published research articles in journals including  *Nature* (2021, 2024, 2026), *Nature Cell Biology* (2018), *Hepatology* (2017).


主要借助于免疫学和分子生物学知识，并整合基因蛋白组学、代谢组学和生物信息学等技术手段，聚焦于抗体基因重组的相关调控机制与外在联系，推动抗体和疫苗设计相关理论和策略的完善及治疗方案的提升。并荣获“哈佛大学华人学者生命科学研究奖”、“上海市优秀毕业生”等荣誉，并被选为博士生代表参加2018年“第68届德国林岛诺贝尔奖医学/生理学获得者大会”；曾获美国癌症研究所（CRI）欧文顿博士后基金资助，担任Frontiers, Fundamental Research等国际学术期刊审稿人。


## Research Interests

Our research will focus on the regulatory networks governing early B cell development, addressing key scientific questions such as chromatin spatial conformational changes and the regulation of loop extrusion. We aim to elucidate the mechanisms by which cis- and trans-regulatory elements, transcription factors, and the microenvironment contribute to the generation of antibody diversity, as well as their applicability in human. This work will provide theoretical support for vaccine design and antibody engineering research. Additionally, we will further explore the therapeutic targets and intervention strategies for diseases related to aberrant antibody gene recombination, such as autoimmune disorders.
